# Worked example: one resume, start to finish

This is the whole method on one resume, from the PDF to the matching LinkedIn. Everything in it is made up: the person, the companies, the numbers and the job post. Any match with a real person or company is a coincidence.

**The person:** Maya Lindqvist, a hospitality graduate (May 2026) in Chicago. She still works part-time as an event assistant, and she only has her resume as a PDF.

**The post:** Event Coordinator at Brightwater Conferences, hybrid in Chicago.

| From the post | Type |
|---|---|
| Bachelor's degree in hospitality, events or a related field | Must-have |
| 1+ year of event or hospitality experience | Must-have |
| Coordinate vendor contracts and logistics for corporate events | Must-have |
| Manage event budgets and track spend | Must-have |
| Experience with event registration platforms | Must-have |
| Strong written communication with clients | Must-have |
| Plan run-of-show and on-site schedules | Responsibility |
| Be the day-of point of contact for vendors | Responsibility |
| Track attendee registration and reporting | Responsibility |
| Bilingual in Spanish and English | Nice-to-have |

## The steps

**Step 0: Pick a direction.** Skipped. Maya only wants event roles.

**Step 1: Gather everything.**
- Claude couldn't open the job board link, so Maya pasted the full post into the chat. It's saved word for word in her Brightwater folder.
- The post also asks for a short cover letter. That's noted for later.
- Maya only had a PDF. Claude pulled the text out of it and built an editable .docx from the template she picked, with nothing typed in by hand.
- There was no bullet bank yet, so this was a first build. Every bullet got rewritten in X-Y-Z with highlighted blanks for the missing numbers, and every line was marked ★:

| Old line | First build | After Maya filled it in |
|---|---|---|
| Responsible for communicating with vendors | ★ Cut day-of vendor delays from [__] to [__] per event after building a shared run-of-show and confirming every arrival [__] hours out | Cut day-of vendor delays from 6 to 1 per event by building a shared run-of-show and confirming every arrival 48 hours out |
| Managed RSVPs for events | ★ Hit [__]% registration for a [__]-guest corporate retreat by reconciling RSVPs and sending the client weekly reports | Hit 96% registration for a 250-guest corporate retreat by reconciling RSVPs and sending the client weekly reports |

- The summary was written fresh in the summary shape and marked ★ too, because her old objective didn't count as an approved summary.
- The box office and Spanish-bookings bullets were new, written from facts Maya gave when asked. Everything stayed ★ until Maya OKed the wording.
- Maya said vendor delays dropped "after" she built the shared run-of-show and started confirming arrivals 48 hours out, so the first build wrote "after", not "by". Claude asked whether those two changes caused the drop or just came first. She said they caused it, since nothing else about the events changed, so the final bullet says "by". The Spanish-bookings bullet got the same question, and she said the families told her they rebooked because of the calls in Spanish.

**Step 2: Check the fit.**
- The post is still taking applications, it's hybrid in Chicago where Maya lives, and she can work in the US without sponsorship. All three pass.
- She meets most of the must-haves. Two are partial (vendor contracts and budgets, which the planner owns) and one is a gap (registration platforms). Two years part-time counts for the 1+ year.
- Her old resume was weak for this post, so tailoring gives her a real edge.

**Step 3: Map the post to proof.**

| From the post | Status | Proof on the page |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's degree in hospitality, events or a related field | Covered | BA, Hospitality Management |
| 1+ year of event or hospitality experience | Covered | Event Assistant since Sept 2024 |
| Coordinate vendor contracts and logistics for corporate events | Partial | Cut day-of vendor delays from 6 to 1 per event. She runs the logistics, and the planner signs the contracts |
| Manage event budgets and track spend | Partial | Saved clients $4K (8%) across 15 weddings by comparing catering quotes. The planner owns the budget |
| Experience with event registration platforms | Gap | Question for Maya. She hasn't used one, so it stays off |
| Strong written communication with clients | Covered | Held client reply time under 1 business day |
| Plan run-of-show and on-site schedules | Covered | Built a shared run-of-show (vendor delays bullet) |
| Be the day-of point of contact for vendors | Covered | Confirmed every vendor arrival 48 hours out |
| Track attendee registration and reporting | Covered | Hit 96% registration, with weekly reports to the client |
| Bilingual in Spanish and English | Covered | Brought in 3 repeat bookings by translating planning calls |

**Step 4: Mirror the true keywords.** "Event Coordinator" became the first summary label. "Run-of-show", "vendor" and "registration" went into the bullets and skills where they were already true. "Event registration platforms" stayed off.

**Step 5: Swap the bullets.**
- 5 bullets for the current role, ordered by what this post wants most.
- The capstone moved from Experience to Projects, because it wasn't paid.
- The barista role came off. Maya decided it didn't help for this post.
- The budget bullet says she compared quotes and flagged overages to the planner, not that she managed the budget.

**Step 6: Fit the page.** The first build ran 4 lines over one page. Tightening the spacing won back 2 lines. Shortening the retreat bullet won back 1, without changing any facts. Cutting the weakest box office bullet won back the last one.

**Step 7: Keep the titles.** Event Assistant matches LinkedIn. "Event Lead" for the capstone was never a job title, so it's now a project name. The file is `Maya Lindqvist - Brightwater Conferences (Event Coordinator).pdf`.

**Step 8: Format the page.** Built in her template .docx at her rules: Calibri, 10.5pt body, 10pt minimum, 0.6 inch margins. The section names follow her field. Her food safety certification moved under Education.

**Step 9: Check, save and show.** All 14 checks passed once Maya had OKed every ★ line. It's one page in the PDF, with no ★ or blank on the page, and every skill has a bullet behind it. Check 10 counts as new rather than unchanged, since this was a first build. Maya saw the PDF before anything went anywhere.

## Then LinkedIn

After Maya saw the PDF, she ran the [LinkedIn playbook](../02-linkedin-playbook/), starting from this resume's decisions:

- **Titles and dates** already matched, except the capstone. It was in Experience as "Event Lead". It moved to Projects, linked to Northfield University with "Associated with", with its skills tagged. It also went in Featured, since the Projects section sits low on the page.
- **Skills both ways.** LinkedIn listed "Event registration software", which she hasn't used, so it came off. It also listed Canva, which isn't on the resume. She used it for the gala invitations, so it stayed on LinkedIn, tagged on the gala project.
- **Barista role.** Off the resume, but Maya kept it on LinkedIn, which has room for more history than one page.
- **Focus Areas** for Event Assistant, with no numbers:
  - Event logistics (run-of-show schedules, vendor arrivals, day-of coordination)
  - Registration tracking (RSVP reconciliation, weekly client reports)
  - Client communication (shared inbox, Spanish-language planning calls)
  - Budget tracking (catering quote comparisons, overage flags)
- **Add only if true:** event registration platforms.
- **Education and honors.** The Northfield entry has no description or Focus Areas. Hospitality Student of the Year stayed in Honors, and the food safety certification went into Licenses & certifications, since recruiters filter by them.
- **Location** changed from "United States" to Chicago.
- **Open to Work** is set to Recruiters only, because she still works at Harbor & Pine.
